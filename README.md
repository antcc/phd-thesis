# A Bayesian approach to functional statistics via reproducing kernel methods: theory and computation

Author: *Antonio Coín*  
Supervisors: *J. R. Berrendero, A. Cuevas* (UAM)

The compiled PDF file of the PhD thesis is available [here](https://github.com/antcc/phd-thesis/releases/download/pdf/thesis.pdf).

## Abstract

Functional Data Analysis (FDA) provides a natural framework for observations that evolve over a continuous domain, which have become commonplace in many statistical applications. Under this point of view, the objects of interest are random functions rather than random points in a finite-dimensional space. Due to its increasing popularity, quite a few statistical methods have been developed for functional data, usually generalizing the existing approaches in the corresponding multivariate problems. However, Bayesian methodology, while crucial in many finite-dimensional models, remains comparatively underdeveloped for many fundamental functional inference problems. 

In this thesis we propose a unified Bayesian framework for functional data based on reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs), with applications to functional regression and functional hypothesis testing. A major aim of this work is to motivate these RKHS models within the functional framework, while also providing efficient techniques to apply them in practice. Although placing a prior distribution on a functional space is generally a hard task, the specific parametric formulation we propose greatly facilitates this. The resulting posterior distribution, which is essential to any Bayesian method, can be approximated computationally through reversible jump Markov chain Monte Carlo (RJMCMC) methods. We focus on functional data represented as a second-order stochastic process $\{X(t): t\in[0,1]\}$ with covariance function $K$. A key assumption common to all models we propose is that the functional parameters of interest are members of the RKHS associated with the kernel $K$. The central idea is to represent functional parameters as finite linear combinations of kernel sections, thereby obtaining tractable finite-dimensional models while retaining a genuinely functional interpretation. These models can be expressed in terms of linear combinations of the marginals of $X$, that is, $\sum_{j=1}^p \beta_j X(t_j)$, by leveraging the correspondence between the RKHS and the linear span of the process. In this representation, the number of components, the coefficients, and the associated time instants (often called impact points) are treated as unknown quantities that are modeled in a Bayesian way.

The first part of the thesis develops Bayesian RKHS-based models for scalar-on-function regression, covering both functional linear regression and functional logistic regression. Priors are assigned jointly to the model dimension, impact points, and regression coefficients, and subsequent inference is performed using RJMCMC, allowing models of different dimensions to be explored within a single Bayesian analysis. The resulting posterior distribution supports several prediction strategies, as well as a Bayesian variable selection procedure based on the marginal posterior distribution of the impact points. In the second part of the thesis we propose a Bayesian one-sample test for the mean function of a Gaussian process, testing an exact point-null hypothesis against a general functional alternative via RKHS approximations. We obtain a closed-form likelihood expression via Radon-Nikodym derivatives, and a spike-and-slab prior assigns positive probability to the null hypothesis while retaining a continuous prior over the alternative. Analytical marginalization of the coefficients largely simplifies the transdimensional posterior computation, which is again performed through RJMCMC. The resulting procedure provides posterior null probabilities and Bayes factors as measures of evidence, together with posterior estimates and simultaneous credible bands for the mean function.

In addition, we develop an asymptotic theory for these Bayesian procedures, as a way to validate the methodology from a theoretical point of view. We study the large-sample consistency of our models and measure the speed of posterior concentration—a standard approach in the frequentist assessment of Bayesian procedures. For functional regression, we establish posterior consistency and contraction rates through applications of classical results by Doob and Schwartz. For the functional test, posterior consistency is achieved under both the null and alternative hypotheses, with polynomial contraction under the null and exponential contraction under fixed alternatives. The theory is also extended to the practically relevant case in which the covariance function is estimated rather than known, showing that suitable plug-in covariance estimators preserve the contraction rates. Our contribution also includes substantial computational work in the form of a RJMCMC sampling implementation for the proposed functional models, which is applied in extensive numerical studies. The results obtained from these experiments highlight the practical usefulness of the methods, showing that our Bayesian procedures are competitive against other usual frequentist alternatives in both simulations and real data sets.


**Keywords**: functional data, Bayesian inference, reproducing kernel Hilbert space, functional regression, functional testing, posterior consistency, reversible jump Markov chain Monte Carlo
